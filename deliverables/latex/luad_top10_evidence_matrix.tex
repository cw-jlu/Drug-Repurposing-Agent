\section{LUAD Top-10 identity and evidence
matrix}\label{luad-top-10-identity-and-evidence-matrix}

Review date: 2026-09-25. This is a research triage artifact, not a
treatment recommendation. The screen used one A549 perturbation
condition (10 µM, 24 h). Replaying the published EH3226 metadata filter
recovered one exact GSE92742 \texttt{sig\_\allowbreak{}id} and \texttt{pert\_\allowbreak{}id} for
every Top-10 row; the crosswalk is
\texttt{configs/\allowbreak{}luad\_\allowbreak{}top10\_\allowbreak{}signature\_\allowbreak{}ids.\allowbreak{}csv}. Cross-source chemical
conflicts with the dated
\href{https://repo-hub.broadinstitute.org/repurposing}{Broad Drug
Repurposing Hub} remain where noted.

\subsection{Candidate-level audit}\label{candidate-level-audit}

\textbf{Rank 1 · hydrocortisone}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Identity status：} Exact source signature
  \texttt{BRD-A75172220}; Hub cross-source stereochemistry differs
\item
  \textbf{Name-level targets / mechanism：} ANXA1, NOS2, NR3C1, NR3C2;
  glucocorticoid-receptor transcription and annexin/eicosanoid signaling
\item
  \textbf{LUAD/NSCLC evidence found：} Hydrocortisone shared
  GR-dependent A549 growth arrest with dexamethasone in vitro
  (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1533045/}{PMID 1533045}); an
  independent network study proposed hydrocortisone computationally for
  NSCLC (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35676421/}{PMID
  35676421})
\item
  \textbf{Evidence against or uncertainty：} GR activation has also been
  linked to chemotherapy resistance and stemness
  (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41344105/}{PMID 41344105}); no
  candidate-specific LUAD animal or clinical efficacy study was found
\item

  chemistry and efficacy unresolved

\subsection{Shared pathway evidence and
contradiction}\label{shared-pathway-evidence-and-contradiction}

Nine of ten names are known or likely glucocorticoids; BRD-K84203638 is
unresolved. This explains a coherent transcriptional class signal, but
it also means the Top-10 is not ten independent mechanistic hypotheses.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Supporting preclinical context: dexamethasone suppressed A549 and
  Calu-1 proliferation through ERK/MAPK and cell-cycle regulation
  (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12204894/}{PMID 12204894}). A
  biomarker-selected study found dexamethasone responses in
  STK11/LKB1-mutant NSCLC models
  (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37035141/}{PMID 37035141}).
  These findings are class-level and biomarker-dependent; they do not
  validate the nine screened compounds.
\item
  Contradicting preclinical context: dexamethasone reduced pemetrexed
  cytotoxicity in GR-responsive nonsquamous NSCLC models
  (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24736075/}{PMID 24736075}). A
  later study linked GR/β-catenin activation to lung-cancer stemness,
  chemoresistance, worse survival signals, and reduced paclitaxel
  benefit (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41344105/}{PMID
  41344105}).
\item
  Clinical-context uncertainty: observational inhaled-corticosteroid
  studies sometimes report lower lung-cancer incidence, but a systematic
  review found no significant effect in the included randomized trials
  (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27761973/}{PMID 27761973}); a
  later meta-analysis rated the evidence low to very low
  (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35843928/}{PMID 35843928}).
  This is prevention evidence in COPD/asthma populations, not LUAD
  treatment evidence.
\item
  Immunotherapy context: retrospective NSCLC studies associate baseline
  or early systemic steroids with worse checkpoint-inhibitor outcomes,
  although indication-related confounding is material and some analyses
  differ (\href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33007977/}{PMID
  33007977}, \href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33431285/}{PMID
  33431285}, \href{https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36142204/}{PMID
  36142204}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Bottom line}\label{bottom-line}

The reconstruction resolves the exact source signature and perturbagen
IDs, but it does not produce a clinically credible Top-10. Cross-source
formulation or stereochemistry conflicts remain for several rows. Only
hydrocortisone and beclomethasone dipropionate have candidate-specific
A549/NSCLC context, and neither has candidate-specific LUAD efficacy
evidence. The appropriate output remains a hypothesis list with
\texttt{insufficient\_\allowbreak{}evidence}. The prespecified dose, subtype,
mechanism and combination plan is in
\texttt{docs/\allowbreak{}luad\_\allowbreak{}experimental\_\allowbreak{}validation\_\allowbreak{}protocol.\allowbreak{}md}.

The search was deliberately bounded to exact names, likely formulation
synonyms, A549/LUAD/NSCLC terms, and shared glucocorticoid evidence.
``No study found'' means none was identified in this bounded review, not
proof that no literature exists.
