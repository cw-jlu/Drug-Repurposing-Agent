\section{TRANSCRIPT benchmark
results}\label{transcript-benchmark-results}

Data: TRANSCRIPT v2.0.0 (613 drugs × 151 diseases; 401 positive, 11
explicit negative, 92,151 unknown associations). Source hashes are in
\href{../data/manifests/transcript-v2.0.0.json}{\texttt{data/\allowbreak{}manifests/\allowbreak{}transcript-v2.\allowbreak{}0.\allowbreak{}0.\allowbreak{}json}}.
The 100-run publication-protocol comparison below uses the
authors\textquotesingle{} NS-AUC metric. The earlier project evaluations
farther down use different seeds, selection rules and global AUC
definitions; their numbers must not be compared directly with this
table.

\subsection{B2 versus the 11 published RECeSS models: 100 runs, five
folds}\label{b2-versus-the-11-published-recess-models-100-runs-five-folds}

B2 was inserted into the pinned official runner, using the same
TRANSCRIPT v2.0.0 input, 100 seed values in the same order, 20\% outer
holdout, five inner folds, row-wise AUC model selection, and
\texttt{Lin\textquotesingle{}s\ AUC} (the paper\textquotesingle s
NS-AUC) calculation as the 11 author-published reference models. Every
metric has 100 finite values. Values are mean ± sample SD; each split
has its own ranking.

B2 ranks 9/12 on random simple and 8/12 on weakly correlated. Against
the strongest reference in each split, its paired mean NS-AUC difference
is −0.2109 versus BNNR (95\% paired bootstrap interval −0.2204 to
−0.2013) and −0.2365 versus MBiRW (descriptive interval −0.2391 to
−0.2338); B2 wins 0/100 paired seeds in both comparisons. The weakly
correlated splitter repeats one outer holdout across its 100 seeds, so
its interval reflects inner-fold/model-selection variation rather than
100 independent drug-group splits.

The authors\textquotesingle{} 22 parameter files use
\texttt{params=null}; the official five-fold operation chooses the best
fold-trained model but does not search hyperparameters. B2 uses its
pre-fixed \texttt{neighbors=10}, \texttt{rrf\_\allowbreak{}k=60}. Its official global
AUC means (0.9446 random; 0.8967 weak) are substantially higher than its
NS-AUC because the upstream global calculation scores the full matrix,
including zero scores outside the validation fold. NS-AUC is the primary
comparison here. One locally rerun LogisticMF seed exactly matched the
published NS-AUC, global AUC and global NDCG. The other ten reference
models use the authors\textquotesingle{} published CSVs rather than
local reruns.

See the \href{recess_official_comparison.md}{full protocol and caveats},
\href{../benchmark/results/recess_official_b2_vs_11.json}{machine-readable
12-model comparison}, and the original B2 result/seed/parameter CSVs in
\texttt{benchmark/\allowbreak{}results/\allowbreak{}recess\_\allowbreak{}official\_\allowbreak{}b2/\allowbreak{}}. This is a comparison
with the paper\textquotesingle s 11-model panel, not a claim against
every later method.

\subsection{Frozen B2 component ablation and prescore LLM
choice}\label{frozen-b2-component-ablation-and-prescore-llm-choice}

The same official runner evaluated B2\textquotesingle s three separate
inputs under the same 100 seeds and five folds. The NS-AUC means ±
sample SD are: B0p 0.5000 ± 0.0000 / 0.5000 ± 0.0000; B1k 0.1747 ±
0.0393 / 0.0562 ± 0.0466; B1 0.4816 ± 0.0340 / 0.5417 ± 0.0000; B2
0.5222 ± 0.0354 / 0.5019 ± 0.0036 (random simple / weakly correlated).
Thus fusion helps relative to B1 on random simple but hurts it on the
repeated weakly correlated outer holdout. A single DeepSeek call,
committed before the component results were inspected, chose B2 for
random and B1 for weakly correlated; these happen to be the best of
these four methods on their respective splits. A new checksum-verified
paired audit
(\texttt{python\ -m\ evals.\allowbreak{}audit\_\allowbreak{}historical\_\allowbreak{}selector\_\allowbreak{}v1}) confirms
that the random choice ties fixed B2 on all 100 seeds and the weak-split
B1 choice exceeds B2 by \textbf{0.039825 NS-AUC} on average (100/100
paired seed values higher; descriptive 5th--95th percentile of seed
differences 0.0361--0.0471). Those 100 weak-split values share one
held-out test set: they are \textbf{not} 100 independent demonstrations
of benefit. Two choices are not a generalization test of LLM-based model
selection, and the comparison remains below strong published models. See
the \href{component_and_llm_adjudication.md}{ablation and LLM
evidence-triage protocol},
\href{../benchmark/results/recess_component_selector_v1_paired_audit.json}{machine-readable
paired audit} with its JSONL trace receipt, and
\href{../benchmark/results/recess_official_component_ablation.json}{component
results}. The separate LUAD LLM triage prioritized research actions, not
clinical treatments or benchmark scores.

A \href{b1k_metric_audit.md}{frozen-seed B1k metric audit} exactly
reproduced one saved seed from each split and found that strict-score
ties dominate its low NS-AUC: 2077/2480 eligible pairs tied in random
simple and 10115/10438 in weakly correlated. The official NS-AUC gives
ties zero credit; half-tie counterfactuals were 0.5639 and 0.5272 on
those seeds, whereas inverted-score strict AUC would be lower. These are
diagnostic values, \textbf{not} alternative official comparison results;
the 100-run primary table above is unchanged.

The weakly correlated split produced identical scores for all
deterministic methods and identical validation label counts across these
three seeds. Inspection of the \texttt{stanscofi} 2.0.1 splitter

input-row-order tie break. The saved B2 runs and this table were
regenerated after that correction.

\subsection{Nested tuning of B1k and
B2}\label{nested-tuning-of-b1k-and-b2}

The new nested runner uses the same three outer seeds and both outer
split protocols. Hyperparameters are selected only by three-fold CV
inside each outer training set; leakage audits in all six JSON files
report zero outer-test coordinates seen by inner CV.

\subsection{Nested tuning of ALSWR, PMF, and
LogisticMF}\label{nested-tuning-of-alswr-pmf-and-logisticmf}

inner selection inside five outer seeds (1234-1238). Each prediction
call receives a feature-identical test object whose rating matrix
contains zeros only. All ten saved runs report zero outer train/test
overlap and zero outer-test coordinates in inner CV.

These earlier project evaluations remain \textbf{limited external
evaluations}. Their grids are intentionally bounded and their values are
not the 100-run publication-protocol scores above. The PMF runner
restores the removed NumPy \texttt{np.\allowbreak{}int} alias. Unknown \texttt{0}
entries are scored as non-positive under the global AUC convention, not
established clinical failures.
